Research Philosophy

While economic theory sees prices as the market equilibrium between supply and demand, market microstructure theory sees price changes as a function of order flows.

$$R_t = f({O}_t) \quad \text{where} \quad R_t = \ln\left(\frac{P_t}{P_{t-1}}\right)$$

Even game theory, as the study of decision-making between independent players, fits into this framework. In plain English, prices move because people trade, and people trade for different reasons.

Some will think of $f$ as some sort of price impact model, and we agree (even though a distribution rather than a point estimate would be more useful for trading purposes). But we prefer to think of $f$ as an abstract function with a parameterization $\theta$, where the overall order flow ${O}_t$ is itself a function of a general market context $c$.

$$R_t = f_{\theta}({O}_t) \quad \text{where} \quad {O}_t = h({c}_t)$$

By $c$, we mean anything that helps explain why an order flow is the way it is. $h$ is how agents react and trade based on $c$.

To be more precise, ${O}_t$ is the aggregation of the order flows from all $n$ agents, each of whom reacts differently to a given context. Formally, we define this as a vector of individual flows:

$${O}_t = ({o}_{1t}, {o}_{2t}, \dots, {o}_{nt}) \quad \text{where} \quad {o}_{it} = {h}_{i}({c}_t) \quad \text{for } i = 1, \dots, n$$

If ${o}^{\text{obs}}_{it} \neq {h}_{i}({c}_t)$, we see it as a lack of information in ${c}_t$, not a lack of complexity in ${h}_{i}$.

In our view, people spend too much time thinking about ${f}$ and not enough about ${c}$; that is, they focus on extracting information from an uninformative ${c}$.

Our research is therefore a directed search over the conditioning space ${c}$. We explicitly frame this as maximizing mutual information $I({O}_t; {c}_t)$.

Each candidate variable to be added to $c$ is measured by how much it reduces the conditional entropy $H({O}_t \mid {c}_t)$ of future flow. This is where $h$ comes in handy, as it can be used as a framework to reason about what is still missing in ${c}$.

We think this level of abstraction is sensible because it applies across asset classes and time horizons, from option market making to global macro trading.

We aim to build systems that adapt under parameter uncertainty, circumventing the overfitting and wishful extrapolation that so often plague backward-looking and rule-based approaches.

What initially spans pages of first-principles reasoning is systematically distilled into essential lines of executable and explanation. If we cannot render it with clarity, we have likely not truly understood it.
